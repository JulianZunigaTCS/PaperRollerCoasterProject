8th Grade Science & Engineering

The Ultimate Energy Rollercoaster

A GANAG Lesson Plan for Physics and Engineering

G Set the Goal

Objective: Students will apply the principles of Potential Energy (PE) and Kinetic Energy (KE) by designing and constructing a functioning paper rollercoaster.

Teacher Talking Point: Begin by explicitly stating the importance of energy in physics. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred. A rollercoaster is the perfect physical manifestation of this rule. Explain that the goal today is to manipulate gravity to maximize ride time—converting high PE into controlled KE.

A Access Prior Knowledge

Before introducing the building materials, engage the class in a discussion to activate their prior knowledge about physics and motion.

  • Ask the class: "Think about the biggest rollercoaster you have ever been on. Why does the ride always start with a massive, slow climb up a giant hill?" (Answer: To build maximum Potential Energy).
  • Ask the class: "What happens if a rollercoaster goes down a steep drop too fast and hits a sharp turn?" (Answer: High Kinetic Energy overcomes friction/gravity, and it derails).
  • Connect these answers to their challenge: They must balance PE and KE to keep their marble on the track for the maximum amount of time.

N Introduce New Information

1. Project Parameters & Constraints:

  • The coaster must fit within a 50 cm x 50 cm x 50 cm area.
  • Materials are strictly limited to paper, masking tape, and paper clips.
  • The evaluation criteria is based on Time. The marble that takes the longest to reach the ground safely wins. More turns, ups, and downs = more time!

2. Show Real Structures Before Building:

Before handing out any paper, show the students what strong paper engineering looks like in real life. Point to the image below on the projector or smartboard and explain the techniques:

Technique Key for Teacher:
• Top Left: Small squared beam (vertical support).
• Top Right: Big squared beam (heavy base support).
• Middle Left: Clip and tape (temporary joint holding before taping).
• Middle Right: Fish mouth (flattened end to wrap around corners).
• Bottom: Sleeve slot (sliding smaller tubes into larger ones to extend length).

3. Distribute Roles & Name Tags:

Explain that real engineers work in specialized teams. Have groups of 4-5 students decide on their roles. Distribute physical name tags (or use the printable templates on page 3) so everyone knows their specific job during the build phase.

Team Roles Overview:

  • Lead Engineer: Reads instructions, manages the 50cm size limit, and plans the physics (PE/KE).
  • Materials Tech: Cuts paper, preps tape, manages paperclips.
  • Tube & Beam Maker: Solely responsible for rolling paper tubes and folding square beams.
  • Assembler: Connects the tracks and beams together using joints.
  • QA / Surveyor: Measures the structure, tests with the marble, checks stability. (If groups of 4, Lead Engineer does this).

A Apply New Information

Allow students to begin prototyping and building. During this phase, the teacher transitions into a facilitator/project manager role.

During the Build:
  • Walk around and ensure the Tube & Beam Maker is actually making parts while the Assembler builds. Stop teams that are all trying to tape the same track at once.
  • Ask groups pointing to their tracks: "Where is the marble moving fastest here? How can you slow it down to get a better time score?"
  • Remind them to use barrier walls on tight corners to prevent the marble from flying off.

G Generalize (Review Goal)

Finally, conclude the activity with testing and presentation. This ties the activity back to the initial Goal of understanding energy transfer.

  • The Measurement: Have the QA/Surveyor from each team prove their structure fits in the 50x50x50 cm limit.
  • The Drop: Time the marble's journey from the release point to the final stopper. Record times on the board.
  • The Explanation (Crucial): Before they sit down, the Lead Engineer must point to the track and explain to the class:
    1. Where the marble has highest Potential Energy.
    2. Where it has highest Kinetic Energy.
    3. How their track design (hills, loops, wide turns) manipulated those forces to slow the marble down.
  • Wrap Up: Summarize as a class how mechanical energy transferred throughout the winning designs.
