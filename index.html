The Ultimate Energy Rollercoaster

The Challenge: Your engineering team has been hired to design a thrilling new rollercoaster using only paper, tape, and paper clips! Your goal is to keep the "passenger" (a marble) on the ride for as long as possible before it reaches the ground.

You must also explain how your rollercoaster demonstrates the principles of Potential Energy (PE) and Kinetic Energy (KE).

📏 The Constraints & Rules

  • Size Limit: Maximum 50 cm tall, 50 cm wide, and 50 cm long.
  • Goal: The longest travel time from start to finish wins!
  • Physics: You must be able to point out where the marble has maximum Potential Energy, maximum Kinetic Energy, and where energy is transferring.

✂️ Materials Allowed

  • Paper (blank or recycled)
  • Masking tape
  • Paper clips
  • Tools: Rulers, tape measures, scissors
  • Formers: Pencils, pens, or markers (used ONLY for rolling tubes, not for building)

Step 1: Assign Team Roles

To work efficiently, assign one of these jobs to each person in your team. (If you have 4 students, the Lead Engineer also does the QA job.)

Lead Engineer

Reads the instructions, divides tasks, and checks the 50cm rules and stability.

Materials Tech

Cuts paper, preps tape strips, manages clips, and keeps the supplies organized.

Tube & Beam Maker

Rolls the paper tubes and folds the beams (V or square shapes).

Assembler

Builds the actual structure by connecting the parts together with tape/clips.

QA / Surveyor

Measures the coaster, checks if it's straight, does stress tests, and times the marble.

Step 2: Physics Planning

  • Potential Energy (PE): When you place the marble at the top (50 cm high), it has maximum Potential Energy.
  • Kinetic Energy (KE): As it drops, energy converts into Kinetic Energy (speed).
  • The Trick: If it drops too fast, the ride is over! Use kinetic energy to send the marble back up small hills, or make it travel in spirals to slow it down. A wider base is better than a narrow top. Sketch your plan before you build!

Step 3: Universal Building Techniques

Use these tested methods to build a strong frame for your track. Reference the visual guide below to see how each technique looks in real life:

Technique Reference Guide

  • 📌 Top Left: Small squared beam - Fold paper into a narrow square tube and tape the edges.
  • 📌 Top Right: Big squared beam - Wider fold for strong base columns.
  • 📌 Middle Left: Clip and tape - Align rolled tubes with a paper clip for temporary holding, then lock with tape.
  • 📌 Middle Right: Fish mouth - Crush and flatten the end of a tube to wrap tightly around a corner.
  • 📌 Bottom: Sleeve slot - Slide one rolled tube directly inside a slightly wider tube to extend lengths.

Pro-Tip (Cross-Bracing): Triangles beat rectangles! Don't forget to add diagonal paper supports (X-shapes) inside your square frames to stop the structure from wobbling.

Step 4: Construct the Track

  • Making Tracks: Cut a piece of paper into 3 or 4 long strips. Fold the long edges up to make "walls". These folded walls become your marble tracks.
  • Continuous Flow: Ensure the end of one track feeds smoothly into the next one. Tape the tracks to your support structure, sloping downwards.
  • Central Column Trick: Roll a thick sheet of paper into a wide cylinder tree trunk. Cut vertical slits at the top to fan it out, and wrap your track in a spiral around it!

Step 5: Test, Adjust, & Fix (QA Process)

  • Test the Run: Drop your marble. Does it go too fast? Add a small uphill slope to slow it down.
  • Control Your Corners: If the marble flies off on a turn, tape a small piece of paper as a "barrier wall" to catch it.
  • The Finish Line: Don't lose your marbles! Build a paper stopper at the very end to catch the ball.

Step 6: Final Evaluation Day 🏁

1. The QA/Surveyor will measure your rollercoaster to ensure it fits the 50x50x50 cm box limit.

2. We will drop the marble and use a stopwatch to time its journey.

3. The Lead Engineer will present the coaster to the class, pointing out exactly where the marble has the highest Potential Energy and the highest Kinetic Energy.
